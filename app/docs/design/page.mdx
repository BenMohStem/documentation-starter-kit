# The Design

<SceneHeader scene="design" height={240} />

<PageHead overline="The constitution" title="The design" />

This page is the design constitution: the rules the world is built to
obey. It is written as **intent** — what the game must do when each
system arrives — and every design decision below is measured against
it. Nothing here describes finished features; that distinction is
never blurred on this site.

The constitution rests on five pillars. Every rule below traces back
to one of them:

## The player

Not a chosen hero. No prophecy, no supernatural destiny, no forced
main quest. The player starts as an ordinary person and *decides*
what to become — hunter, farmer, fisherman, shepherd, carpenter,
blacksmith, miner, engineer, trader, explorer, soldier, mercenary,
knight, commander, politician, governor, scientist, inventor, ruler —
or remains a simple traveler forever. **Exploration itself is the
primary motivation.**

## The world

A large procedurally generated world, inspired by Earth but entirely
fictional: mountains, valleys, plains, forests, deserts, rivers,
lakes, oceans, islands, caves, underground regions, coastlines, snow
country, fertile regions, dry regions. Structure like Minecraft
where useful, but terrain smoother and more natural — closer to
Valheim's feel.

**Verticality is real.** The world is divided by continuous altitude
and geography — never by portals. Going underground means physically
descending: caves, underground rivers, minerals, geological
formations, underground animals, dangerous environments, ancient
ruins, extremely deep regions. Going up means climbing or flying. The
Moon is reached one day by actual technological progression — a real
celestial destination, not a teleport.

## No magic

No spells, no mana, no gods as gameplay entities, no supernatural
powers, no teleportation, no fantasy crafting ingredients, no
arbitrary fantasy physics. Everything has a physical or technological
explanation. **If something seems extraordinary, the player can
investigate it** rather than be told a supernatural cause.

## AI is the heart

Every important entity has goals, needs, memory, relationships, and
decision-making. A villager needs food, water, shelter, safety,
employment, family, social contact. A merchant monitors prices, buys
cheap, travels to profitable markets, avoids dangerous roads, reacts
to war. A soldier follows orders, fears, protects allies, retreats
when badly hurt. A ruler manages taxes, armies, treaties, rebellions.

**Animals are real organisms** — deer, wolves, bears, horses,
elephants, cattle, sheep, goats, pigs, chickens, fish, birds — with
hunger, fear, aggression, reproduction, territory, predator/prey
relationships, environmental preferences, movement patterns. A bear
behaves like a bear and can outrun a human. The world is never
balanced around player convenience.

## Combat

Physical and consequential. Damage depends on weapon type, velocity,
impact location, armor, penetration, angle, anatomy, sharpness, and
force. A sufficiently powerful strike to an unprotected vital can
kill immediately. Armor protects the areas it actually covers.
Weapons behave differently: spear = reach, sword = cutting, mace =
blunt trauma, axe = chopping, dagger = penetration, bow/crossbow =
projectiles with distinct mechanics, shield = protection and impact
surface. **Combat is never artificially safe for balance.** Skill and
preparation matter.

Mount & Blade-style participation: fight personally, ride, command
formations, issue orders, scout, retreat, capture enemies, defend
castles, negotiate with commanders, become a commander, eventually a
ruler. **The player is one participant inside the battle, never an
omniscient camera.** Horses and large animals have stamina, speed,
acceleration, health, temperament, training, carrying capacity — and
their physical size affects combat.

## Building, engineering, crafting

Simple building rules, Minecraft-inspired, creative combinations
over hundreds of arbitrary recipes. The engineering system is
redstone-like in spirit — switches, pressure plates, levers, buttons,
wires, logic gates, sensors, timers, pistons, motors, gears, shafts,
pumps, generators, batteries, relays, signal transmitters — combinable
into automatic farms, doors, elevators, traps, sorting machines,
factories, calculators, logic circuits, simple computers, robots.

**Computers emerge, never appear**: logic gates + memory + clock +
input + output, assembled by the player who can understand how the
machine works. Robots likewise: sensors + motors + power + control
logic + structure. Progression: stone tools → metallurgy → mechanical
engineering → electricity → automation → computing → robotics → space.

Crafting is grounded: ore → smelting → metal; wood → processing →
planks; sand → heating → glass; metal → machining → components.
Never "3 magical rocks + 1 flower = machine."

## Farming, economy, civilization

Farming is systemic: soil preparation, planting, irrigation,
fertilization, harvesting, storage, animal husbandry, selective
breeding, crop rotation, automation. Economies are real: production,
consumption, supply, demand, prices, transportation, labor, taxation,
trade, shortages, surpluses — war affects the economy, drought
affects food prices, a mine creates wealth, a destroyed road
disrupts trade, a new technology changes production.

Civilizations are cultures, not stat-bonused factions: languages,
laws, political structures, technologies, military traditions,
architecture, relationships, beliefs, values. Cities grow and
decline; settlements are founded; kingdoms split; wars begin; peace
treaties occur; trade routes develop; political factions compete.
Diplomacy runs on interests — trade agreements, alliances, vassalage,
tribute, prisoner exchanges, marriages, espionage, embargoes — never
fixed scripted outcomes.

**War emerges from the simulation**: resources, territory, politics,
trade, revenge, ideology, security, leadership. Armies require food,
equipment, horses, logistics, money, manpower — and never teleport.

## Technology and science

Technology emerges through experimentation and civilization
development. The player can discover unusual paths; there is no
forced linear tree. Science is discovering relationships — materials,
mechanics, chemistry, electricity, biology, astronomy, geology — by
experimenting. Never an unlock screen: the player discovers a
material conducts electricity and *uses that knowledge* to create
something new.

## UI and the HUD

Minimal. **The world itself communicates information whenever
possible.** Clear interfaces exist only for inventory, crafting,
building, engineering, diplomacy, army orders, economy, and the world
map. Avoid excessive HUD elements. Noticing is the mechanic.

## Graphics direction

Minimalist, stylized, low-poly, pixelated textures, Valheim-inspired
simplicity. **Excellent lighting, strong atmosphere, readable
silhouettes, beautiful skies, dynamic shadows, day/night cycle,
weather.** Volumetric-looking lighting where practical. Explicitly
not photorealistic. The world should look simple but impressive:
excellent light + strong composition + simple geometry + beautiful
atmosphere.

## Performance as a core feature

Level-of-detail simulation, spatial partitioning, entity sleeping,
population abstraction, distance-based AI updates, procedural
generation, instancing, pooling, multithreading where appropriate,
deterministic simulation where useful. Hierarchical simulation:
nearby animal = full AI + physics; nearby village = individual
citizens; distant city = statistical population; distant civilization
= economic abstraction. Detail increases as the player approaches —
honestly, measurably.

## The moral physics

The world contains a subtle philosophical layer — existence, order,
consciousness, morality, freedom, responsibility, cruelty,
compassion, knowledge, the origin of the universe. **Never preached,
never stated, never required.** Morality is an emergent consequence:
a civilization that treats people brutally gains short-term
efficiency and eventually suffers rebellion, economic damage,
population decline, distrust, crime, emigration, instability. One that
protects people develops trust, stability, productivity, cooperation,
innovation. Not a good/evil meter — arithmetic, applied over time.

## Success

The prototype succeeds the day a player discovers an unscripted
interaction: *"I built this machine, which affected this farm, which
changed this economy, which changed this settlement, which changed
this political situation."* That feeling of emergence is the defining
feature. Above all: **the world must keep living even when the player
does nothing.**
