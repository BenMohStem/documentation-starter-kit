# The Engine

<span className="wwa-page-title">The engine</span>

Beneath the world there is an engine, written from scratch in C —
physics, rendering, sound, input, the build system, even the standard
functions it runs on. Owned end to end, and measured at every step.

**Why build it instead of using one — not as a protest, as a
strategy.** When every layer is yours, nothing in the stack is
mysterious: performance comes from understanding, not from luck, and
a bug is yours to find, in your own code. A world with tens of
thousands of living, colliding, eating, trading things cannot be
simulated honestly on an engine you cannot see inside.

<div className="wwa-docs-section">

### The floor — standard layer & builder

Memory, strings, math, I/O, threads — written by hand, tested
adversarially against designed edge cases, known vectors, and
property-based sweeps. The build system is native C too: it knows the
dependency graph, hashes content, and refuses to rebuild what has not
changed. Hundreds of thousands of automated checks stand under
everything else.

</div>

<div className="wwa-docs-section">

### Physics

The classical core done carefully — fixed timestep, exact overlap
tests, impulse resolution with friction — plus a research program:
warm-started contacts that replay near-instantly and exactly; the
discovery that *where bodies sit in memory* changes how fast the
solver can think; the convergence behavior of relaxation methods. The
physics is treated as a research subject, and its claims survive
oracles, corrupted controls, and repetition.

</div>

<div className="wwa-docs-section">

### Renderer

A tile-based software rasterizer: triangles sorted into screen tiles,
exact per-pixel depth, promoted only after passing a per-pixel oracle
across adversarial scenes. The target aesthetic is set: simple
geometry, excellent light — the beauty of the world will come from
composition and atmosphere, not asset density. Faster paths that
failed honest speed bars were kept out — deliberately.

</div>

<div className="wwa-docs-section">

### Sound, input, jobs

Generated audio (no recorded assets), deterministic input snapshots
(the same inputs always produce the same world), and a job system
that spreads work across the machine — with grain sizes measured, not
guessed.

</div>

<div className="wwa-docs-section">

### The discipline that binds it

Every subsystem earns its place the same way: measured on the
reference machine, compared honestly against alternatives, kept or
recorded as a lesson. The instruments themselves are audited — twice
a promoted result turned out to be a measurement artifact, was
retracted within the session, and made the rules stronger. The full
record of that discipline — every benchmark, incident, retraction —
is the [Research library](/research).

</div>
