## The research program, in one paragraph

Contacts are a graph; the solver walks it every frame. The program
asked, in order: can contacts *remember* (warm-start — yes,
70.98× replay at zero drift)? Can the graph be *reordered* for the
cache (layout management — sometimes, 3.11× on hub topologies at
256K bodies, and *never* universally)? Can the solver *converge
faster* (PSOR with workload-dependent relaxation — promising, one
case 50.4M → 15.4M cycles, no production promotion yet)? Each
question was answered by measurement, and each negative is kept.

## What was learned the hard way

- **A "2× faster" claim once died to fresh-process repetition** —
  the spread *between* runs was larger than the claimed effect. The
  promotion was retracted the same session, and the noise-floor rule
  was born;
- **The instrument is audited before it measures** — one layout
  benchmark silently capped its declared churn at ~534 of 12,288
  contacts. The stated workload never ran. The corrected benchmark
  reversed the conclusion;
- **No technique wins everywhere** — the most repeated finding in
  the whole record. Every promotion carries its scope: where it
  wins, and where it is known to lose.

> Nothing about the physics is claimed on vibes.
