The plan follows a simple conviction: **the world can only be as
honest as the engine underneath it.** So the engine comes first — not
all of it, but everything the world's first promises will stand on.

## The order

1. **The floor** — a from-scratch standard layer and build system,
   tested adversarially. When every layer is owned, nothing in the
   stack is mysterious, and performance comes from understanding
   rather than luck.
2. **The physics core** — bodies, mass, friction, contact — running,
   replaying deterministically, and staying correct while thousands of
   them interact.
3. **The senses** — one shared spatial sense of *what is near what*,
   serving physics, sound, sight, and AI alike, so the world can
   afford to be full of things that notice each other.
4. **The world's first systems** — ecology, resources, animal
   behavior: the isolated experiments connected at last into one
   living place.
5. **Civilization** — needs, work, trade, settlements: the beginning
   of an economy that can price a war.
6. **Engineering, kingdoms, history** — machines from levers to
   computers; war, diplomacy, consequence; and far up, one day, the
   Moon — reached the only way that means anything, by building the
   machines that climb.

## How each step is chosen

Not by feature lists — by leverage and by proof. A step is taken when
two things are true: the world needs it (the design cannot honestly
proceed without it), and the engine can carry it (measured, not
assumed). Ideas from research papers are hypotheses; the reference
machine decides. Techniques that lose are recorded, not retried
blindly.

## What "done" will mean

Not a finished game in the commercial sense — a vertical slice of a
living world: one region where the ecology runs, the economy prices,
people work and trade and feud, machines can be built, and a player
who walks away for an hour returns to a place that changed without
them. Everything past that is the same discipline, applied further.

No dates are promised. The rule is: measure, then trust; promote, then
build. The order of the world follows the order of proof.
