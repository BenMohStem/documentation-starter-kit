## The family

One generic quantizer (`wwa_fx_quant/dequant`, parameterized by
eb/mb/bias/nan_mode, round-to-nearest-even, union-based type punning)
instantiates the whole family: FP16, BF16, FP8 E4M3/E5M2, FP6 E3M2/E2M3,
FP4 E2M1, packed FP4/FP6, MXFP8/6/4 (32-value blocks with a shared E8M0
scale), and NVFP4 (16-value blocks with E4M3 block scale + FP32 tensor
scale). Every conversion is sign-preserving through round-trip; the
FP operation tree is part of the numerical contract (thread C doctrine:
bitwise-identical lanes, no FMA contraction surprises, reproducible
reductions).

Bugs caught by the self-tests before promotion: MXFP8/MXFP6 sign-bit
loss from a destructive post-conversion mask; FP4/FP6 sign-bit
placement; the OCP E4M3 448/0x7E boundary.

## The consumer verdict (the honest headline)

The 2.55/2.95 consumer-kernel benchmarks — quantization in the *actual*
kernel, not just conversion microbenchmarks — are why nothing was
promoted for compute:

## The standing conclusion

Low precision is a **storage and transport** research surface — packed
renderer packet consumption, adjacent scale/data layouts — while FP32
and BF16 remain the compute paths. Posit16 stays in-tree as research,
explicitly unsupported. No universal low-precision claim survives the
reference machine, so none is made.
