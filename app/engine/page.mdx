Beneath the world there is an engine, written from scratch in C —
physics, rendering, sound, input, the build system, even the standard
functions it runs on. Owned end to end, and measured at every step.

## Why build it, instead of using one

Not as a protest — as a strategy. When every layer is yours, nothing
in the stack is mysterious: performance comes from understanding, not
from luck, and a bug is yours to find, in your own code. And a world
with tens of thousands of living, colliding, eating, trading things
cannot be simulated honestly on an engine you can't see inside.

The engine also sets the ceiling for the world: how many creatures can
think, how many bodies can rest on each other, how far a signal can
travel before the frame ends. Every month of engine work is a promise
the world can later keep.

## What is inside today

- **The floor** — the standard layer: memory, strings, math, I/O,
  threads, tested by hundreds of thousands of automated checks;
- **Physics** — bodies with mass and friction, warm-started contacts,
  and a research program into the solver itself;
- **Renderer** — a tile-based software rasterizer with an exact
  correctness oracle, stepping toward 3D worlds;
- **Sound, input, and jobs** — generated audio, deterministic input
  snapshots, and work distributed across the machine.

Each piece earns its place the same way: measured, compared honestly
against its alternatives, kept or recorded as a lesson.
