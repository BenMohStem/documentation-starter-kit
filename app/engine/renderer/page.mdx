<span className="wwa-page-title">The renderer</span>

The renderer's job is the quiet part of the world's beauty: light,
depth, and distance — simple geometry, honestly drawn.

## What exists today

A tile-based software rasterizer: the screen divided into small
tiles, triangles sorted into the tiles they touch, each pixel's depth
checked exactly. It renders the current first-person demonstration,
and it was promoted only after passing a per-pixel oracle across a
battery of adversarial scenes — any wrong pixel is a failed test.

## The choices, and why

- **Tiles** because they keep memory traffic local — the engine's law,
  applied to drawing;
- **Software first** because a renderer you can audit per-pixel is
  the right foundation for one you later accelerate;
- **Simple geometry, excellent light** is the target aesthetic:
  minimal, readable, atmospheric — the beauty of the world should come
  from composition and light, not from asset density.

Several faster paths were tried and not shipped: some SIMD interiors
passed correctness but failed the honest speed bar; a quantized
variant stayed a research note. The kept-off list is as deliberate as
the promoted one.
