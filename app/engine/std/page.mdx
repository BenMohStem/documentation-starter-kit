## Bugs the tests caught (kept as knowledge)

- **`wwa_strerror`** — `memcpy` copied 15 bytes of a 14-char literal,
  copying the NUL into `buf[14]` and truncating every unknown-error
  message. gdb's `finish` "fixed" it and sent the project chasing ghosts;
  disassembly at `0x140018fb0` proved itoa innocent. Lesson:
  `memcpy(dst, "literal", N)` requires `N == strlen(literal)`;
- **`wwa_utoa` truncation** — kept least-significant digits instead of
  most-significant (2³² base-2 printed as "000...0"), found by a
  property-based round-trip sweep;
- **`wwa_dbl_pow2` subnormals** — the n < −1022 branch constructed
  mantissa bits wrongly;
- **`wwa_strtod`** — rewritten from double accumulation to u64 integer +
  single scaling, fixing 17-digit parsing;
- **`%e/%g` accuracy** — fixed via `wwa_fmt_pow10()`.

## The test method

Hardcoded constants (sin(π/2)=1), algebraic identities over tens of
thousands of iterations (sin²+cos²=1 over 20,000), round-trips
(sin(asin(x))=x), NaN propagation (NaN ≠ NaN), argument-reduction
boundaries near π/2 multiples, ±0.0 and subnormals, and FNV-1a
known-vector + avalanche checks. Hashing is validated by single-bit-flip
avalanche (~50% of output bits), streaming-vs-oneshot equality, and
8-thread counter exactness.

## The honest gap

Tolerances are absolute eps, not ULP-based; physics and rendering have
no self-test coverage beyond the AVX2 bit-exact check and bench timing.
These gaps are documented, not hidden — ULP-based comparison is the
registered next step.
