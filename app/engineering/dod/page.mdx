## The idea

Data is laid out the way it is **accessed**, not the way it is thought
about. A contact solver does not care that a "Body" is a conceptual object
— it sweeps `invm, ix, iy, iz` and velocity tuples in contact order. The
engine's arrays reflect the sweep.

## The measured pieces

- **SoA / AoSoA access domains**: hot solver state lives in
  structure-of-arrays packets with block-local grouping (AoSoA), sized to
  the cache hierarchy;
- **The 224.4 → 45.6 cycles/contact spread**: the 3.174 measurements showed
  the same solver ranging 224.4 (identity layout, scattered access) to 45.6
  (re-blocked layout, access-local) — layout is worth **~5x** on the solve
  side. That spread is why layout management is a research thread at all;
- **Color-local packets**: contacts grouped by graph-color batches so SIMD
  lanes read contiguous packets — the coloring contract guarantees
  independence, the layout guarantees locality.

## The current frontier

The [layout research](/research/layout) turned the static layouts into a
**dynamic manager**: when the world churns, local repair and measured
topology now decide whether, where, and how to reorganize — with the O(1)
cheap-swap primitive as the first promoted building block.

## What it is not

DOD here is not "no structs" or "cargo-cult SoA". It is the doctrine that
**layout follows access, access is measured, and organization cost is
charged**. The layout manager is that doctrine, running.
