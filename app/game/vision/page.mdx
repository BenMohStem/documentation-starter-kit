# The Vision

World Without Answers is a game about **a world that does not explain
itself**.

- No tutorial. No quest markers. No dialogue that tells you what things mean.
- No notification queue, no engagement loop, no fear of missing out.
- Processes run because of rules: water flows downhill, trees grow where
  soil is, animals eat because they are hungry.

The player who looks closer finds more world. That is the whole mechanic.

## What kind of game?

A first-person sandbox in a large procedural wilderness:

- **Wander** — valleys, forests, rivers, weather, night and day;
- **Observe** — animals with real needs and routines; plants that grow,
  spread, and compete;
- **Interact physically** — everything has mass; you can build, break, dam,
  dig, carry, and the world answers with consequence, not narration;
- **Seek** — the world is built so a careful observer can find signs of
  order: evidence, in the world's own terms, that it is the work of an
  intelligence. The game never states a conclusion. It never quizzes you.

## The wager

> A small number of understandable rules, faithfully applied, every frame,
> can produce a world that feels alive — without a single scripted event.

That wager is the engineering program. Every system below is measured before
it is believed:

- **Physics**: 6-DoF contacts, friction, stacking, warm starting — a native
  C solver where every optimization claim survived an exact-state oracle
  ([Research](/research)).
- **Spatial foundation**: physics, AI, audio and rendering share one
  locality layer — measured ~5.7x vs duplicated grids on the host workload.
- **Emergence**: metabolism, regrowth, stigmergy, homeostatic drives —
  prototype mechanisms measured in deterministic laboratories, not yet a
  living world (we say so plainly).
- **Later, honestly**: ecosystems, economy, farming, crafting, building
  mechanics, societies, war. Each arrives only when its mechanism survives
  measurement. The [Roadmap](/game/roadmap) shows the order and the honesty
  rules.

## What it is not

- Not an MMO. Not a service game. Not procedurally cluttered.
- Not a physics toy with nothing to find.
- Not a promise of dates we cannot keep.
