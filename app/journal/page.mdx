invalidated ancestor visible.

## Selected entries, in the project's own words

### 3.174 — Preparation must be paid

A solver reorganization measured a 4.9x solve speedup and lost anyway: its
preparation cost was 5.2x. The complete-path cost law was born, and every
promotion since charges organization + remap + maintenance against savings.

### 3.178–3.179 — The amortization model

`T_amortized(H) = (T_prepare + T_remap)/H + T_solve(H)` — the horizon H over
which an organization must repay itself. Initially published with an
incorrect amortization computation, then **corrected in public**: the
corrected tables changed the decision. The error stays visible.

### 3.180 — The oracle that compares itself

A churn benchmark was caught comparing an oracle against its own state
rather than an independent reference — the defect was found, disclosed, and
the negative-control discipline extended.

### 3.185–3.187 — The noise incident and the honest win

The full arc lives in [Research](/research/layout): a promotion caught
being noise, an instrument rebuilt, and the first result that survived every
gate.

### 3.189 — The audit that caught its own author

A chain-detection probe was designed, its predictions written down, and
then — before measuring any policy — the probe was measured itself
against all seven topology families. The audit refuted the predictions:
grids and hubs walk through the contact array too, and only the *shape*
of the walk (index-local vs scatter-jumping) separates them. The rule
was fixed, re-registered, and only then allowed to gate results. The
same session caught a contaminated timing battery (background runs on
the pinned core) by cross-checking against a clean anchor and re-running.
The instruments are now audited like the claims.

### 3.190 — Five bugs, one answer, one correction in public

The maintained degree table was built to be array-order-independent —
and it entered testing with four defects that its own gates caught: a
deletion scheme that wrapped a counter, an allocation tax, a hash that
put half a grid's edges in one slot, and a record-stream bug that had
made the audit itself lie. Every number was re-derived after each
fix. With the clean instrument the 3.189 open question closed — the
1% miss was the old probe's, not the world's — and the chain scope
extended.

Then, after the report shipped, the fifth catch: a back-of-envelope
cost model refused to accept a measured 110M-cycle rebuild that
arithmetic said should cost ~10M. The edge table had been allocated
64× larger than its registered size — a defect no gate could see,
because content is capacity-independent and every policy was priced
against the same inflated table. At the registered size the prices
collapsed (+10–15 became +2–4) and one published verdict flipped: the
mix battery the report had conceded to the old probe, the selector in
fact wins against every static at every churn. The correction was
published with the error left visible, and the cost model became the
fifth standing gate. The deepest lesson of the iteration is not about
hash tables at all: **a measurement that flatters your expectations
deserves the same suspicion as one that contradicts them.**
