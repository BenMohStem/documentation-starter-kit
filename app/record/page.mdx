# The Record

import { Cards } from "nextra/components";

This section is what makes the project unusual: the **permanent,
append-only record of its own claims** — promotions, negative results,
retractions, and the doctrine that decides between them.

The project's history is preserved in its own words, in its own files,
with its own numbers. Nothing is cleaned up for presentation. When a
measurement invalidated an earlier claim, both the claim and the
invalidation stayed in the record.

## Why keep failures?

Because in simulation research, the most expensive mistakes are the ones
you don't know you made. Every incident in this record was caught by a
gate built from an earlier incident; every negative result is a wall
that stops a future session from walking the same dead end:

- The LOD reordering that lost (2.30× *worse*) is why grouped-contiguous
  could be trusted when it won at 0.41×;
- The adaptive spatial manager's state-integrity failure (2.78) taught
  the design constraint the successful layout selector (3.188) obeyed;
- The 3.185 noise artifact is why every promotion since carries a noise
  floor and two independent runs;
- The 3.190 record-pair bug that silently corrupted *every* degree
  measurement is why the audit envelope is now stated honestly as 0–10%,
  not "5/5 through 20%".

The failure trail **is** the diagnosis record. That sentence is the
project's shortest self-description.
