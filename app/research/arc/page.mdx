# A Full Loop: The Layout Manager

The clearest demonstration of what the corpus is *for*: the first
engine iteration driven end-to-end by the literature pipeline — from
four paper notes to a promoted, scoped, measured engine mechanism.

## Hypotheses from the corpus

| Paper | What it contributed |
|---|---|
| Roy 2012 — Memory Hierarchy Sensitive Graph Layout (arXiv:1203.5675) | Two-level hierarchical blocking; the piggyback-re-layout idea |
| Fauzia et al. 2014 — Beyond Reuse Distance Analysis (arXiv:1401.5024) | Why current-order histograms underestimate reordering potential; the headroom idea — v1 honestly rejected as non-discriminatory |
| Satav & Singh 2022 — Locality-based Graph Reordering (arXiv:2111.12281) | The reorder-cost/exec-time trade-off framing; community-based layouts |
| Shanmugavelu et al. 2024 — FP Non-Associativity (arXiv:2408.05148) | Independent confirmation of the numerical-contract doctrine |

Each note recorded what was actually used — and what was rejected.

## The loop, closed

1. **3.185** — the corpus-grounded policies (hierarchical blocking,
   repair-with-reblock, epochal headroom) run in the first full layout
   manager test. A 1.44× promotion is **retracted the same session**
   (noise incident 3.185-1);
2. **3.186** — the instrument is rebuilt (interleaved, min-of-K, noise
   floors). The corrected instrument says: no policy beats identity on
   that workload — and *why*: the natural order was already optimal by
   construction. The workload itself was fixed next, not the claim;
3. **3.187** — adversarial topology families built so natural order is
   genuinely hostile; the first honest, scoped promotion lands (O(1)
   cheap-swap on hub topologies). The expensive greedy repair from the
   literature framing *lost* to a primitive that costs almost nothing —
   kept in the journal;
4. **3.188–3.190** — the promotion becomes an online decision policy
   (starts as identity, must earn its layout, never loses on hostile
   families), extends to chains at 128K via the audited degree probe,
   and its price is measured and published.

## What the papers did NOT decide

The corpus supplied the *hypotheses*: blocking, headroom, community
layout. The engine's own measurements decided: the headroom trigger was
rejected; hierarchical blocking lost to an O(1) swap; the final
architecture — probe, decline-to-act, amortized rebuild — is not in any
of the four papers. That is the use policy working as designed, and it
is the standard every future thread (solver, broadphase, world
simulation) is held to.
