## Papers are hypotheses; measurements decide

The project runs a local research corpus — papers are never authority.
A paper may show an optimization winning on its workload; WWA's
benchmark must show it winning on the 5600H, the actual data layout,
and the actual workload. If it doesn't, the result is kept — labeled,
not deleted.

Every thread's reading guide ranks papers by WWA relevance — not by
prestige — with each top paper carrying a note skeleton: claim, method,
assumptions, **what WWA can reuse, what WWA must NOT copy**, threats to
validity, and the proposed WWA experiment with its oracle.

## The pipeline

1. **Harvest** — arXiv keyword-targeted per thread, checkpointed, one
   process at a time (two concurrent = timeouts — a measured failure
   that became a rule), every step appended to an audit log;
2. **Rank** — relevance scoring against the live engine threads;
3. **Read** — deep-read structured notes (the template demands the
   threats and the must-not-copy columns);
4. **Decide** — hypotheses enter the pre-registration → gates → verdict
   pipeline; the corpus never decides.

Failures are knowledge here too: OpenAlex is parked (HTTP 429), the
arXiv pacing rules and http→https upgrade were all learned the hard way
and written down in the corpus log.
