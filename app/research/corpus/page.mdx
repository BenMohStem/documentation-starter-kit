# The Corpus

<SceneHeader scene="research" height={230} />

<PageHead overline="Research library" title="The literature corpus" />

The project keeps its own research library: papers harvested with
provenance, ranked by relevance to the engine's live problems, and
read adversarially. The corpus is organized into eight threads —
each thread matched to a problem the engine was actually facing
when the thread was opened.

The relationship runs one direction: **papers are hypotheses; the
machine decides.** Every note records what the project can reuse,
what it must not copy, and the threats to the paper's validity.
Where a thread fed an engine iteration, the trail is kept — from
paper to hypothesis to gate to verdict in the
[ledger](/research/ledger).

## How the harvest is governed

The corpus has its own operational law, learned from real failures:

- One arXiv process at a time — two concurrent harvests deadlock;
- Rate-limited sources are parked and retried politely, never hammered;
- Every file carries its sha256, metadata, and extraction state — provenance is not optional;
- Failures are knowledge: the harvest log keeps them with dates.

## The loop that makes it worth keeping

The corpus exists for one loop: **read → hypothesis → pre-registered
experiment → gate → verdict.** The layout-manager program is the
loop's deepest run — papers from thread B became the policies of
3.185, the policies failed the fresh-process gate, and the failure
became the doctrine that now governs everything. The papers did not
build the engine. They made the questions sharper.
