# Dynamic Layout Management

import StatusBadge from "@/components/status-badge";

The live research thread (iterations 3.185 – 3.189), driven by the project's
research corpus: papers from the local library became hypotheses, the
hypotheses became benchmarks, and the benchmarks became decisions.

## The problem

The engine's solver sweeps over contacts, touching two bodies per contact.
When the world churns — bodies move, contacts form and break — the physical
order of bodies in memory stops matching the access pattern of the solver.
The layout manager must decide, every frame: **repair locally, rebuild
globally, or leave it alone?**

The standing law (from 3.178): an organization is worth it only when

> predicted solver savings + quality benefit > organization + remap +
> maintenance + migration

## The arc, era by era

### 3.185 — First full test, and a caught mistake

All policies tested at once: never-reorder, always-rebuild, span-triggered
rebuild, local repair, repair-with-reblock, and a two-level hierarchical
blocking layout (from Roy 2012, arXiv:1203.5675 — found in the corpus).

A span-triggered policy measured 1.44x better at 10% churn. It passed the
oracle, the negative control, and the sanitizers. It was promoted.

**Then the promotion was retracted the same session.** Fresh-process
repetition showed the noise between processes (59–302 cycles/contact) was
larger than the claimed effect. The promotion was a measurement artifact —
incident 3.185-1, the 3.157 benchmark-hallucination class, caught by the
reproducibility gate.

### 3.186 — The instrument, rebuilt

- **Interleaved measurement**: every frame, all policies run back-to-back on
  the same mutation epoch — sharing thermal and scheduler conditions;
- **min-of-K + noise floors**: the spread is measured and printed next to
  every claim; a promotion must exceed 2x the noise floor;
- The corrected instrument confirmed: on that workload, **no policy
  separated from never-reorder**. The 1.44x was noise.

And a foundation lesson: the legacy contact builder was **index-local by
construction** — natural order was already optimal, so the workload could
never test layout managers at all. At 128K bodies, reordering actively
destroyed locality.

### 3.187 — Adversarial topologies and the first honest promotion

Five topology families built so that natural order is hostile:
chain-scattered, grid-2D, random-sparse, hub-stars, articulated. The results
matrix is now complete:

| Topology | Does layout management help? |
|---|---|
| Hub/community (stars) | **Yes** — beyond-noise win, promoted |
| Scattered chains | **Yes at scale** — promoted in 3.189 (128K bodies, low churn), directional at 32K |
| Grid-2D, articulated, legacy | No — natural order is correct |
| Random contacts | Neutral — solve gains buried by organization cost |

**The promotion**: on hub-structured topologies at low churn, a BFS-family
layout plus an **O(1) cheap-swap repair** (pull a mutated contact's
endpoint next to its partner) beats never-reordering by 30–40
cycles/contact — verified at 5 repetitions, noise floors 4.8–13.2, all gates
passing (exact-state oracle, negative control 5/5, ASan+UBSan clean,
warning-clean build). Scoped, labeled, honest.

The expensive greedy repair from 3.185 lost to a primitive that costs
almost nothing — the error-arc is preserved in the journal.

### 3.188 — The manager arrives

The 3.187 scoped promotion became an online **decision policy**:
adaptive_selector probes the live contact array each frame (a stride
sample through an open-addressing repetition table), and only on hub
evidence does it pay a one-time BFS rebuild and switch to the O(1)
cheap-swap repair. It starts as identity — **it must earn its layout** —
and it rebuilds identity when the hub signature dies. No oracle access,
no schedule access, every cost self-timed.

The safety property is the point: on every non-hub family it **declines
to act** and matches never-reorder — while static reordering policies pay
the wrong-layout tax instead, from a few cycles on grids to 10–25 on
articulated families and far worse beyond. Promoted after the full
gate battery (oracle, negative control, sanitizers, fresh-process
repetition).

### 3.189 — The instrument audited, chains earned, scale verified

Three results:

**The probe audit before the claims.** A new chain probe was designed,
pre-registered, and then validated alone — no policy timings — against
all seven topology families under two mutation models. The audit
**refuted its own pre-registered predictions**: grids and hubs measure
as walks too (consecutive contacts sharing an anchor), so the walk
signal alone cannot identify a chain. A third clause was added — is the
shared-id trace index-local (grid) or scatter-jumping (chain)? —
re-registered before any policy data, and the matrix went 5/5 on every
family. The instrument that failed its audit never gated a claim.

**Chains promoted — scoped.** With the validated probe, the selector's
chain mode wins on scattered chains at 128K bodies: 26.9 and 26.7
cycles/contact over two independent runs against a bar of ~13. At 32K
the same action is only directional — the win is cache-hierarchy-dependent,
and the promotion says so.

**Scale verification.** The hub promotion holds and grows: at 256K
bodies (working set beyond all cache) identity degrades to 156
cycles/contact while the selector stays flat at 50 — a 3.1× win,
beating every always-policy. And on the mixed six-family schedule, the
selector matches identity everywhere **and** beats every static
reordering policy at every churn level: no-universal-best, made
operational.

### 3.190 — The graph instrument, the 1% answer, and the price of maintenance

Four results, one correction in public:

**The 1% question answered.** 3.189 closed with an unresolved miss: the
chain action won at 0% churn but lost at 1% — was that the instrument
or the physics? The maintained degree table (distinct-partner degree
per body, updated from the mutation stream) is array-order-independent
by construction, and under it the selector holds chain mode the whole
48-frame horizon at 1% churn and clears the promotion bar ~9× over,
twice. **The miss was instrument-bound.** The chain scope extends to
0–1% churn at 128K bodies.

**The walk probe's decay was half-real.** At 5% churn the clean trace
shows the chain genuinely dissolving — degree-1 endpoints rise
monotonically from frame ~20 to 22% by frame 47 as both copies of path
edges flip away. The selector exits honestly, but the exit is
economically premature: static policies keep winning on the stale
layout. 5% stays unpromoted, and the measured mechanism chain
(dead-edge tombstone accumulation, post-exit maintenance, threshold
flapping) sets the next iteration's pre-registrations.

**A published verdict, corrected in public.** The iteration first
shipped with the mix battery reading "the maintained instrument loses
to plain identity by 11–23 — upkeep breaks amortization." Then
cost-model re-analysis refused to believe a 110M-cycle table rebuild
that arithmetic said should cost ~10M — and found the edge table had
been over-allocated 64× versus its registered size at every scale. At
the registered size the rebuild is L3-resident, the never-lose price
collapses from +10–15 to +2–4 cycles, and the mix flips: the selector
beats every static policy at every churn level, and identity only at
the two highest churn points by +2–3. The error stays visible; the
fifth gate joins the law set: **every published cost number must
survive a back-of-envelope cost model, because content gates and
symmetric pricing cannot see an overallocation.**

And the failure-preservation rule earned its keep throughout: five
implementation defects caught in total (a tombstone-less deletion that
wrapped a degree counter, a per-rep allocation tax of 548M cycles, a
hash that collapsed half of all grid edges into one slot, a
record-stream bug that corrupted every degree measurement, and the
overallocation) — four by gates built from previous incidents, the
fifth by refusing to believe a number. Every published number is
re-derived post-fix; content verdicts were capacity-independent and
never moved.

## What this buys the game

A solver that adapts to the world's own structure. Piles of debris,
articulated creatures, clustered buildings — community-shaped physics —
get managed locality automatically; open scattered scenes stay untouched.
The manager is conditional, because **no layout is universally best** —
that is now a measured fact, not a slogan. And the next lesson is
priced: **instruments are not free** — the engine's contact manager
will own its own change events, so the degree table is the
engine-realistic design; its maintenance cost is now a measured design
axis, not a surprise.
