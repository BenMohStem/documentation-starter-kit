## The rules

1. **A paper is a hypothesis source, never an authority.** Speedups
   reported on power-law social graphs do not transfer to contact
   graphs. Every reused idea re-measures on WWA's own topology
   families, scales, and churn — or it does not ship;
2. **Every experiment defines its oracle before it runs** — exact-state
   or high-precision independent reference, plus a negative control
   that must fail if the oracle is real, plus registered workloads
   (bodies, topologies, churn, horizons);
3. **Every promotion criterion is pre-registered**: predicted savings
   must exceed organization + remap + maintenance — the complete-path
   cost law, charged in full;
4. **Results return as WWA iteration files** with their links recorded
   in the corpus database — the paper trail and the measurement trail
   stay joined;
5. **Negative results are permanent evidence.** A benchmark that loses
   is labeled and kept forever, so no future session re-tries the same
   idea for the wrong workload.

## The note template

Every deep-read paper fills a structured note: claim, method,
assumptions, **what WWA can reuse**, **what WWA must NOT copy**
(non-determinism, workload-dependence claimed as universal,
memory-traffic unaccounted, preparation cost uncharged), threats to
validity, and the proposed WWA experiment. The must-not-copy column
exists because the corpus's greatest value has been learning *what not
to import* — the LOD negative, the SoA anti-slogan, and the rejected
headroom trigger all saved months.

## Why this is unusual

Most projects cite papers to justify decisions. This project cites
papers as *defendants*: each one is read adversarially, tested on the
reference machine against its own stated assumptions, and released with
a verdict. The corpus is the engine's immune system for ideas.
