# Emergence

<span className="wwa-page-title">Emergence</span>

The whole design rests on one bet: **a small number of honest rules,
applied faithfully every frame, produces a world that feels alive** —
without anyone scripting it.

Twenty systems that truly interact beat two thousand decorative items.
Animals with hunger, fear, and memory; plants that grow, get
over-hunted, recover or don't; economies where a war raises the price
of bread; machines made of levers and gears that can be combined into
calculators. Nothing is placed for the player to find. Everything is
a consequence.

## How we keep ourselves honest

Because "emergence" is the easiest word to abuse, we hold it to the
same standard as the physics: every mechanism is first built as a
small, deterministic experiment with a measurable outcome — a
population that must stabilize, a food web that must hold, a signal
field that must decay at a known rate. It runs, it is measured, and
only then does it enter the world. The first experiments — energy
budgets, regrowing resources, environmental signals, needs that stay
stable without attention — have already been run in isolation.

## What it will mean to play it

You will notice things. The crows gather where someone left grain. The
wolves avoid the lit road. A merchant's prices tell you about a war
you never saw. And because the world never announces its rules, the
knowledge is yours — earned by watching, the way it is in the real one.
