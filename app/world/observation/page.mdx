## Noticing is the mechanic

Understanding must be earned. The world does not narrate itself: no quest
markers, no objective log that plays the game for you, no floating
exclamation points over the heads of people who need things. What you
learn, you learn by watching — the way the crow calls its friends when it
sees you, the way the rabbit's holes cluster where the soil is soft, the
way merchants' prices move when the road is unsafe.

The player's loop is deliberately slow:

> **action → consequence → observation → hypothesis → experiment → discovery**

Science in the world follows the same law: relationships are discovered
by experimenting, not unlocked on a screen.

## Why the engine comes before the world

An observation can only be honest if the world producing it is real. A
scripted crow that "calls friends" when a trigger volume fires is a
costume; a crow that calls friends because it saw you, remembered you,
and has a flock is a fact about the simulation. That difference is why
this project spends years on physics, locality, and determinism before
animals: the world must be able to keep its own promises.

## The demo's honest position

Today's playable demo demonstrates the *engine's* honesty, not the
world's: what you see in [the demo](/demo) is real physics stepping,
real input, deterministic replay. The observation mechanics — animal
memory, signal fields, civilization drift — are measured prototypes (see
[Emergence](/world/emergence)) and will enter the world the same way
everything else does: through a gate.
