# The Premise

## A world that does not explain itself

The name is a constraint, not a riddle. There is no oracle, no script, no
designer placing the answer where the player will find it. The world must
generate its questions and its answers from the same material — bodies
that move, signals that decay, structures that persist because they are
useful, not because they were authored.

You begin as an ordinary wanderer with almost nothing: no prophecy, no
supernatural destiny, no forced main quest. You can become a hunter,
farmer, craftsman, engineer, scientist, explorer, soldier, merchant,
lord, ruler — or remain a traveler forever. The most important rule:

> **The world must keep living even when the player does nothing.**

Return from a long journey and the farms have grown, the animals have
moved, the prices have shifted, the war you left behind has its own
history. The world does not pause for the player.

## What it is not

- **No magic.** No spells, no mana, no gods as gameplay entities, no
  teleportation. Everything that happens has a physical or technological
  explanation, and extraordinary things are investigable.
- **No drug loop.** No casino mechanics, no FOMO, no loot boxes, no
  daily-reward hooks. The intended closing thought is *"what a useful
  experience that taught me something"*, not *"when can I play again"*.
- **No HUD hand-holding.** The world itself communicates; noticing is the
  mechanic. Clear interfaces exist only where reality needs them
  (inventory, crafting, maps, orders).
- **No content bloat.** Twenty highly reusable components over two
  thousand meaningless items.

## The quiet layer

The world contains a hidden depth about existence, order, and morality —
never preached, never stated, never a quest. Morality is not a meter: a
brutal civilization reaps rebellion and decline; a kind one earns trust
and stability — through the simulation's own consequences, not through
messages. The wonder is the point: the world is vast, lawful, and
understandable, and what the player does with that understanding is left
entirely to the player.

## Success, defined

The success criterion is a moment no script can produce: a player builds
a machine that alters a farm, that shifts an economy, that changes a
settlement, that changes a political situation — and realizes nobody
authored that chain but the rules themselves. A beautiful world with
shallow simulation is **not** the goal; a modest world with astonishing
systemic interaction **is**.
